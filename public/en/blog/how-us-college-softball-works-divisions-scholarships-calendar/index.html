How U.S. college softball works: divisions, scholarships, calendar and championships

Published November 3, 2026

College softball is split between the NCAA (three divisions), the NAIA and the NJCAA (junior colleges, also with three divisions), and scholarships vary a lot by level. Of the 187 open needs we collected as of November 5, 2026, 61% are from junior colleges, where there is more room for an international player.

Who runs what

Women's college softball is run by several associations. The NCAA has three divisions: Division I is the most visible and competitive, Division II has partial scholarships and Division III gives no athletic scholarships. The NAIA is a separate association with its own championship. The NJCAA groups junior colleges, two-year institutions that are also split into D1, D2 and D3: these are their own divisions and do not match the NCAA's. In California (CCCAA) and the Northwest (NWAC), junior colleges belong to different associations.

Championships: how the 2026 season ended

  • NCAA Division I: a 64-team tournament (31 conference champions and 33 committee selections). Regionals May 15 to 17, super regionals May 21 to 24 and the Women's College World Series May 28 to June 4 in Oklahoma City. Champion: Texas, which beat Texas Tech 7-3 and 4-1 in the final. Wikipedia: 2026 NCAA Division I softball tournament.
  • NAIA: 48 teams. Opening round at 10 sites May 11 to 15, double elimination, and a World Series May 21 to 28 in Columbus, Georgia. Champion: Madonna (Michigan), which beat Southeastern (Florida) 8-4. Wikipedia: 2026 NAIA softball tournament.
  • NJCAA and NCAA D2/D3: they also hold national championships, but we could not confirm the format and do not detail it.

How recruiting works

In Division I, coaches can start contacting players on September 1 of junior year, according to our note on contact dates. In Division II they can contact you at any time by phone, message or email, and the NAIA and junior colleges do not follow NCAA dates. According to NextCommit: how to get recruited for college softball, the 2026 signing date for Division I and II scholarship letters is November 11; confirm it at ncaa.org. Showcases (PGF Nationals, Triple Crown, Alliance Fastpitch, among others) are a window for coaches. If you finish high school in December, see the recruiting timeline to order your steps.

What this means for an international player

This is our reading of the data, not a guarantee. Three out of four needs (76%) are from junior colleges or the NAIA, and only 3 are from NCAA Division I: those are the levels recruiting most right now. NJCAA Division I offers full scholarships, and many players later transfer from a junior college to a four-year school. Pitchers and catchers add up to 52% of the needs, although every program and year is different. Before writing, read the guides and check eligibility.

Limits of this data

These are coach posts, days or weeks old: the spot may be filled. They are not every vacancy, because many coaches recruit without posting. Of the 60 rosters we could read, 19 players list a hometown outside the U.S. (13 programs) and none from Argentina or Latin America; for the other programs we could not read the roster. NAIA and NJCAA Division I scholarship figures come partly from a recruiting source and should be confirmed.

Frequently asked questions

Do junior colleges have Division I, II and III?

Yes. The NJCAA has its own Divisions I, II and III, separate from the NCAA's. Division I can give full scholarships, Division II covers tuition, fees and books but not room and board, and Division III gives no athletic scholarships.

How many scholarships does a softball team have?

In NCAA Division I, since 2025-26 there is no scholarship cap but a roster limit of 25 players; in Division II the maximum is 7.2 equivalencies; Division III gives no athletic scholarships. The NAIA and the NJCAA have their own rules: confirm them on the official sites.

When does the college softball season start?

In 2026 it started January 23 in the NJCAA, January 30 in NCAA D2, February 5 in D1, February 6 in the NAIA and February 13 in D3. Cold-weather programs start later.
