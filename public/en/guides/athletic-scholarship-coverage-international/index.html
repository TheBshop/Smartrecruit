How much does a U.S. college athletic scholarship cover?

Updated: October 31, 2026

A "full ride" is rarely just athletic: it usually combines an athletic scholarship, academic merit aid and money from the university itself. Knowing this in advance avoids frustration.

Athletic scholarships by division

NCAA Division I. Since 2025, programs have roster limits instead of scholarship caps, and each university decides how much to offer. Full scholarships exist, but they are highly competitive.

NCAA Division II and NAIA. Almost always partial scholarships. The coach splits a total amount across the whole roster.

NCAA Division III. No athletic scholarships. Aid comes from academic merit or the university, and it can be generous.

Junior college. Cheaper, some offer scholarships, and many players later transfer to a four-year school.

What does not apply to international students

International students cannot receive U.S. federal aid (Pell grants, federal loans). They can receive athletic scholarships, academic merit scholarships and aid from the university itself.

How to calculate what your family pays

Total yearly cost (tuition + housing + food + insurance + flights) − athletic scholarship − academic scholarship − university aid = what your family pays.

Good grades and a strong score on an exam such as the SAT or the Duolingo English Test raise the academic scholarship, which is often added on top of the athletic one.

What to ask the coach about money

How much does the offer cover, and for how many years? Is it renewed automatically? Can I add academic scholarships? Which costs are not covered? What is the final net cost for an international student? Always ask for the offer in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Do international students get athletic scholarships in the U.S.?

Yes. International athletes can receive athletic scholarships, academic merit scholarships and university aid, but not U.S. federal aid.

Which division gives full scholarships?

Division I can offer full scholarships and decides how many to give under roster limits. Division II and NAIA mostly give partial scholarships, and Division III gives no athletic scholarships.

What is an equivalency scholarship?

In sports with equivalency limits, the coach splits a total number of scholarships into smaller portions across the whole roster, so many athletes receive partial awards.

Should I accept an offer without seeing it in writing?

No. Ask for the amount, the duration, the renewal conditions and the costs not covered in writing before you decide.
