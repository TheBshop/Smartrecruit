How to make a highlight video for U.S. college recruiting

Updated: October 31, 2026

Almost no coach recruits an international athlete without seeing them play. The email opens the door; the video decides whether they reply.

The basics

Length: 3 to 5 minutes. Coaches watch the first 30 to 60 seconds and decide whether to keep going, so put your best plays first. Opening: a 5 to 10 second title card with your name, position, graduation year, height, club, country and email. Identify yourself: before each play, mark who you are with an arrow, a circle or a one-second pause. Use real games over training sessions, against the best opponent possible. Skip loud music and effects.

What to show by position

Goalkeepers: reflex saves, crosses, footwork, one-on-ones and communication. Defenders: recoveries, anticipation, aerial play and clean distribution. Midfielders: line-breaking passes, vision, winning the ball and arriving in the box. Forwards: different kinds of goals, runs, pressing and assists. Basketball: reading the game, shooting, defense and transition. Volleyball: the full action of your position, including setting or passing.

Also add one complete, unedited game: many coaches ask for it after the highlights.

Where to upload it

YouTube (set to Unlisted if you do not want it public), Hudl or Vimeo, with a clear title: "First Last · Position · Class of 2028 · Country". Check that the link opens without asking for permissions, and update it every season.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a recruiting highlight video be?

Between 3 and 5 minutes, with your best plays in the first 30 to 60 seconds.

Should I include a full game?

Yes, as a separate link. Many coaches ask for a complete game after watching the highlights.

Where should I upload my video?

YouTube (unlisted if you prefer), Hudl or Vimeo, with a title that includes your name, position, graduation year and country.

Do I need to pay someone to edit it?

No. A clear, simple edit with identified plays works better than heavy effects.
