Baseball scholarships in the U.S. for Latin American players

Updated: October 31, 2026

Baseball is the men's college sport equivalent to women's softball: there are programs in every division and scholarships in most. If you are a man who plays softball, this is the closest athletic route; if you play baseball, the process is the same as for any other sport.

First: men's softball does not exist in college

NCAA, NAIA and NJCAA softball is women's only, with no scholarships for men. For a man who plays softball, the athletic route is baseball (or another sport he plays). The skills overlap in part but are not the same: pitching and hitting change quite a bit, and coaches will want baseball video. Fielders usually adapt more easily than pitchers.

Why look at junior colleges

Junior colleges (NJCAA and regional associations) are two-year schools that recruit many international players, cost less and, in NJCAA D1, can give full scholarships. Transferring to a four-year university afterwards is common. It is a reasonable route if your video is not yet strong enough for the NCAA.

Eligibility and video

NCAA D1 and D2 require the Eligibility Center, and the NAIA has its own: see the eligibility guide. In baseball, video and numbers weigh heavily: pitch velocity, 60-yard or home-to-first time, catcher pop time, exit velocity.

If you want to keep playing softball

There are college softball clubs with no scholarships or professional coach, but that is not a recruiting path. For financial help you are left with academic and merit scholarships, which can be combined with playing on a club team.

Frequently asked questions

Are there men's softball scholarships in the U.S.?

No. NCAA, NAIA and NJCAA college softball is women's only. The men's equivalent is baseball.

Can a softball player get a baseball scholarship?

He can try, especially as a fielder, but coaches will ask for baseball video. Pitching and hitting differ from softball.

How many scholarships does a baseball team have?

It depends on the division: 9 equivalencies in NCAA D2, 12 in the NAIA, none in D3, and in NCAA D1 a 34-player roster with no scholarship cap for programs that opted in to the House settlement.
