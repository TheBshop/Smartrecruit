Sports recruiting agency vs doing it yourself: an honest comparison

Updated: October 31, 2026

Smartrecruit is a website for getting a U.S. college sports scholarship on your own, for free. An agency charges to do part of that work for you. Here are the differences, when each path makes sense and what to ask before paying.

The short answer

College coaches' emails are public, and so are the openings they post. What an agency usually sells is time, organization and guidance. If you are willing to do the process yourself, you can do it for free; if you prefer to delegate, an agency can be an option, but check carefully what it includes.

When doing it yourself makes sense

If you can spend a few hours a week, are comfortable writing short emails in English with a template, and want to understand each step (eligibility, video, follow-up), the do-it-yourself route is the cheapest and keeps you in control.

When an agency can make sense

If you have no time or desire to manage the process, want someone to guide every decision, or your situation is unusual (a transfer, a complicated eligibility case), an advisor can help. In that case, choose carefully.

What to ask an agency before paying

1. What exactly does the price include and exclude? 2. Can I see real examples of athletes with my profile and where they ended up? 3. Who writes to the coaches, and can I see the emails sent? 4. What happens if I get no offers: is there a refund? 5. Do you charge universities or athletes? 6. What contract do I sign, and for how long?

What you have to do either way

With or without an agency, the athlete must register with the eligibility center, keep their grades up, have a good video and reply quickly to coaches. Start with the guides on eligibility, video and how to email a coach.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an agency to get a U.S. college scholarship?

No. You can contact coaches directly, and many prefer to hear from the athlete. An agency is an option, not a requirement.

Is Smartrecruit an agency?

No. It is a website for doing the process on your own, for free. It is not an agent or an intermediary, does not guarantee scholarships or admission, and is not affiliated with the NCAA, the NAIA or any university.

How do I know if an agency is trustworthy?

Ask for the contract and price in writing and for verifiable examples of athletes they represented, and be wary of guaranteed-scholarship promises or full upfront payments without clear conditions.

Do coaches reply more when the email comes from an agency?

There is no rule. A short, personalized email written by the athlete, with video and key data, often works well. What matters is that it is clear and shows real interest in that program.
