How to email a U.S. college coach

Updated: October 31, 2026

Coaches receive hundreds of emails. Yours has to be readable in 30 seconds and give them a concrete reason to open your video.

The structure

1. Who you are: position, graduation year, country and club. 2. Why that program: something specific (an open spot, the major, the conference). 3. Your key numbers: height, stats, GPA. 4. The link to your video. 5. A closing with one concrete question. Keep it to 150 to 200 words.

The subject line

Short and informative: Class of 2028 GK | Juan Pérez | Argentina | Highlights.

Example

Subject: Class of 2028 GK | Juan Pérez | Argentina | Highlights

Hello Coach Smith,

My name is Juan Pérez, a goalkeeper from Argentina (Class of 2028) playing for Club Atlético Example. I am very interested in [University] because of your [major] program and your team's style of play.

- Height: 6'2" (1.88 m)
- GPA: 3.6 (8.7/10 in Argentina)
- Highlights: [link]

I would love to know if you are still recruiting goalkeepers for the 2028 class and what the next steps would be.

Thank you for your time,
Juan Pérez
[email] · [WhatsApp]

Follow-up

If there is no reply, write again after 10 to 14 days with something new: a new video, a game, a grade update. After that, write whenever you have news to share.

Common mistakes

Sending the same email to everyone without personalizing it, long emails, no video link, not stating your graduation year, and writing to the coach of a different team at the university. International athletes should also state their GPA in U.S. terms or explain their grading scale.

Frequently asked questions

How long should an email to a college coach be?

Between 150 and 200 words: who you are, why that program, your key numbers, your video link and one concrete question.

When should I follow up?

After 10 to 14 days without a reply, and only with something new to share, such as a new video or a game.

Should I email the head coach or an assistant?

Either can work. Replies often come faster from assistant coaches, who usually handle recruiting. Always write to the coach of your own sport and gender.

Do I have to write in English?

Yes. U.S. coaches expect English, and an email in another language is often skipped.
