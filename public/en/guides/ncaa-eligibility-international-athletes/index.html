NCAA and NAIA eligibility for international athletes: step by step

Updated: October 31, 2026

A coach wanting you is not enough: an official body has to confirm that you meet the academic and amateurism requirements. This guide explains what to do and when, with a focus on athletes from outside the United States.

Which center do I register with?

If you want to play in…You register with…
NCAA Division I or IIThe NCAA Eligibility Center (eligibilitycenter.org), academic and athletic certification account
NCAA Division IIIInternational students still need to certify amateurism through the athletic-only account
NAIAThe NAIA Eligibility Center (playnaia.org). It is separate: being registered with the NCAA is not enough
Junior college (NJCAA)There is no eligibility center, but you must have finished high school

NCAA account types and what they cost

Profile Page (free). Start here. It gives you an NCAA ID, which is worth putting in every email to a coach.

Academic and athletic certification account (USD 170 for international students). You need it for Division I or II, and it must be complete before official visits or signing. Most athletes pay for it when a coach shows real interest.

Athletic-only account (USD 75). For international athletes planning to play in Division III. Fee waivers depend on U.S. benefit programs, so in practice a student at a school outside the U.S. does not qualify.

NAIA in four steps

1. Create your profile at playnaia.org. 2. Your school sends your academic records to the NAIA Eligibility Center. 3. As an international student you buy the InCred evaluation (incredevals.org): only what InCred verifies counts. 4. When an NAIA coach adds you to their recruiting list, your case is evaluated with priority. International registration costs USD 170. To be eligible you need a 2.3 GPA or higher, or two of three: ACT 18 / SAT 970, a 2.0 GPA, or the top half of your class.

Timeline if your school year ends in December

In Argentina, Uruguay, Chile and Paraguay high school ends in December, and U.S. classes start in August (or January for the second semester). Two years before graduating: create your Profile Page (and an NAIA profile if you are interested). A year and a half before: request your transcript from your school and find out how to get an official English translation. When a Division I or II coach gets serious: move to the paid account and upload your documents. At graduation: send the final certificate and proof of graduation; without them the evaluation cannot close.

The 5-year rule (Division I)

Since 2026, Division I gives you five seasons within five years. The clock starts with your first full-time enrollment at a college or the academic year in which you turn 19, whichever comes first, and it does not stop if you do not play or transfer. A long gap year after high school can cost you seasons, so plan to enroll the August after you graduate. Division II keeps the rule of four seasons in ten semesters.

Amateurism: where international athletes get tangled

In many countries youth teams and the first team share one club. The NCAA asks about signed contracts, payment beyond expenses or equipment, training or playing with a professional squad, and agents. Always answer truthfully: a false answer is far worse than a situation that can be explained. If in doubt, ask the NCAA before you register.

Rules change often, so confirm the current version on the official NCAA and NAIA websites.

Frequently asked questions

Do international students have to register with the NCAA Eligibility Center?

Yes, if they want to play in NCAA Division I or II, and also to certify amateurism for Division III. The NAIA has its own center, and junior colleges (NJCAA) have none.

How much does it cost to register as an international athlete?

The NCAA Profile Page is free. The academic and athletic certification account costs USD 170 for international students and the athletic-only account USD 75. NAIA international registration costs USD 170.

When should I start the eligibility process?

About two years before you finish high school. Create your free profile first and pay for the certification account when a coach shows real interest.

Can a gap year hurt my eligibility?

In Division I it can: the five-year clock keeps running even if you do not play. Plan to enroll the August after you graduate.
